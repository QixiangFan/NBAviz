# Clean data

Generated by `python build_clean_data.py` from the raw cache in `data/raw/` (regular season only): team and league tables from Basketball-Reference ([Kaggle `sumitrodatta/nba-aba-baa-stats`](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/sumitrodatta/nba-aba-baa-stats) v56), shots and players from NBA.com. Do not edit by hand.

## Files

| Column | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `season` | Starting year (2025 = 2025-26) |
| `season_label` | e.g. 2025-26 |
| `team_games` | Team-games played league-wide |
| `ppg` | Points per team-game |
| `fg3m_pg` | 3-pointers made per team-game |

| `fg3a_rate` | Share of field-goal attempts that were threes |

| `shortened` | Why the season was short (lockout / COVID), else blank |

## Cleaning rules

1. Team and league stats come from Basketball-Reference's season totals (Kaggle, pinned version), which are complete from 1979-80, the first season with a 3-point line. NBA.com's game logs leave 3PA blank for most games before 1985-86, so they are only used for team ids and to cross-check games and wins. League rows sum every team's totals; pace and offensive rating are Basketball-Reference's estimates.
2. Each Basketball-Reference team-season gets NBA.com's franchise id by season and team name (NBA.com's "LA Clippers", from 2015-16, is Basketball-Reference's "Los Angeles Clippers"). Relocations and renames (Seattle → Oklahoma City, New Jersey → Brooklyn, Charlotte/New Orleans) stay one franchise; labels use NBA.com's abbreviation for that season, as in the player tables.
3. One game was cancelled (Indiana at Boston, 2013-04-16), so Boston and Indiana have 81 games in 2012-13; NBA.com lists it with no result and it is left out of the cross-check.
4. Lockout and COVID seasons (1998-99, 2011-12, 2019-20, 2020-21) are kept but flagged; every comparison uses per-game rates or win %, never season totals.
5. To compare teams across eras, shooting stats are also given as z-scores within their own season.
6. Shot charts: team files for franchises that did not exist that season are empty and skipped; heaves from beyond half court are dropped from the grid but counted when checking totals.
7. Shot zones are rebuilt from the shot charts and must agree with the shot type. NBA.com draws its zones with today's 23.75 ft arc, so in 1996-97, when the line was 22 ft all around, 19,165 threes (a tenth of all shots) are labeled Mid-Range. That inflated the season's mid-range share from 29.8% to 40.0%. Threes outside a 3-point zone are moved to the corner or above-the-break zone by location, and twos inside one to Mid-Range. Left and right corners are combined; backcourt heaves are excluded.
8. Players: traded players have one combined row under their last team. The radar only ranks players with 500+ minutes and 20+ games, against the same season's players.
